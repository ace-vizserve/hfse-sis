## Classroom — the section × term staff workspace

### KD #160

Classroom module — one `section × term` workspace, as a **peer** module (2026-07-29). Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-28-classroom-workspace-design.md`. Built in 8 phases, all on `main`.

**Why.** Teaching work is organised around a class; the app was organised around modules. Four separate section-list surfaces existed (`/sis/sections`, `/markbook/sections`, `/attendance/sections`, `/evaluation/sections`) — four independent implementations of the same entity. The schema already agreed: `grading_sheets` is unique on `(term_id, section_id, subject_id)`, `attendance_records` on `(section_student_id, term_id)`, `evaluation_writeups` on `(term_id, student_id)` via the roster. Only the navigation didn't. `components/sections/section-row-actions.tsx` had already been patched with one-way "Open attendance"/"Open write-ups" hops from Markbook rows — the friction was real and previously worked around in the narrowest place.

**Scope correction — students and parents are NOT users of this system.** Students have no login; parents only reach the read-only report-card Bearer API on the external admissions portal. Every classroom feature premised on a student/parent consumer (resource/worksheet libraries, announcements, assignments, parent messaging, seating charts, behaviour records) has **no audience here and is not in scope, ever**. Classroom is a staff workspace.

**Shape.** `Classroom = section × term`; subject is a dimension _inside_ it, never part of its identity. A `section × subject` classroom would show attendance to a subject teacher who cannot read it (see capability, below) and would give that teacher one classroom per section — more destinations, not fewer. It is a **peer** module, not a parent: Markbook/Attendance/Evaluation keep all their cross-section surfaces (report cards, change requests, analytics, bulk import, virtue themes, a subject teacher's cross-section sheet list), which a parent/child nesting would orphan. Two axes over the same data — module = "attendance across all classes", classroom = "everything about P4 Obedience, Term 2". Same precedent as `/records/students` vs `/records/students/[id]`.

**Who it's for is settled by RLS, not preference** (`005_rls_teacher_scoping.sql`): `attendance_records` and `report_card_comments` are gated `is_adviser_for_section` — **form adviser only**; `grading_sheets`/`grade_entries` use `is_teacher_for_sheet` (own subject, or every subject if adviser); `students`/`section_students` use `is_teacher_for_section` (any assignment). So a **subject-teacher-only user physically cannot read a section's attendance or write-ups** — Classroom renders a narrowed view for them (Overview + Grades + Students, no attendance/write-up routes at all) rather than panels that would come back empty. **FCA and subject teacher are different jobs sharing one RBAC role (`teacher`)** — the distinction lives in `teacher_assignments`, not in `Role`.

**`lib/classroom/scope.ts` is the single source for that decision.** Pure, unit-tested. `resolveClassroomScope(role, assignments)` → `{ sectionIds, capabilityBySection, isOversight }` with capability `adviser | subject | oversight`; `canReadAttendance` / `canReadWriteups` / `canReadRoster` mirror the RLS predicates above. **Two invariants with tests pinning them:** `sectionIds === null` means "all sections" (oversight) while `[]` means "none" — a caller conflating them shows every class to a role with none; and adviser beats subject in the same section **regardless of row order**, since DB row order is arbitrary.

**Routes.** `app/(classroom)/` route group + layout mirroring the other module layouts. `/classroom` (class list, scoped) and `/classroom/[sectionId]` as a hub with sub-routes — Overview, Grades, Students, Attendance, Write-ups, Timeline, Settings — following the Academic Summary hub pattern (KD #134) so each is deep-linkable and independently gated. Term axis on `?term_id=`, preserved across tabs, resolved via `lib/classroom/terms.ts::resolveSelectedTermId`, which **delegates to the canonical `resolveCurrentTermId` + `sgToday()`** rather than adding a sixth divergent term implementation.

**Authorization is belt-and-braces, deliberately.** `ROUTE_ACCESS` gates only the `/classroom` prefix, so every sub-route is directly reachable by URL by any allowed role. The layout resolves capability and `notFound()`s when null, **and `attendance/` and `write-ups/` each check `canReadAttendance`/`canReadWriteups` independently** — a layout-only check does not protect a typed URL, and those two are the RLS-restricted ones. Sub-nav never renders a tab the viewer cannot open.

**Health + Timeline** (read-only, no schema). Health composes `computePublishReadiness(service, sectionId, termId)` — the richest per-(section × term) payload that already existed — and surfaces **which** class lacks an adviser, the per-section detail `lib/sis/readiness.ts::fetchAdvisers` computes and then discards to a `.size` (the AY pill says "advisers 8/10" and nobody could see which two). Timeline filters `audit_log` by **indexed `entity_id`** over five gathered sources: the section id, its grading-sheet ids, its `section_students` ids, its students' `evaluation_writeups` ids, and the roster's **enrolee numbers** (transfers are keyed by `enroleeNumber`, not a `section_students` id — `entity_id` is `text` since migration 043). The enrolee source is **paired with a tight action allowlist** (`ENROLEE_TIMELINE_ACTIONS`) because the same enrolee number keys every `sis.*` admissions action; matching id-only would turn a teacher's timeline into an admissions feed. **Per-mark attendance is deliberately excluded** — `attendance.daily.update`/`.correct` log with `entityId: null` and `section_student_id` in unindexed `context` jsonb, at roughly one row per student per school day (~1,800 for a 30-student class per term). Do not "fix" this with a `context->>` filter; the right move would be a per-submission summary row.

**Settings is deliberately tiny.** Student display order (client-persisted, no schema) + a **private per-teacher class note** — the plan's only migration, **094**. `classroom_notes` is `(section_id, teacher_user_id)` unique, section-scoped not term-scoped, RLS `select` where `teacher_user_id = auth.uid()` with **no oversight bypass** (unlike every other scoped-read policy in this codebase, a school_admin cannot read another teacher's note); writes denied to `authenticated` and done via a service-role route that takes `teacher_user_id` from the **verified session, never the request body**. The audit row logs `length`, never `content`. "Show grade colours" and "show running average" were **dropped** — nothing in Classroom renders a grade or an average, so they'd be dead switches.

**Nothing policy-shaped may ever go in Settings.** From the original feature vision, all rejected with reasons: "Default Score 0" violates Hard Rule #3 (Blank ≠ Zero — `null` is excluded from numerator _and_ denominator); "Round Grades"/"Auto Calculate" violate Hard Rules #1–2 (`transmute()` hardcodes `Math.floor` per DepEd, and `lib/compute/quarterly.ts` throws on import unless the canonical case returns 93); custom excused reasons need a CHECK migration and are school policy (KD #48); "Late after 8:15" has no data behind it (`attendance_daily` stores a manually-chosen status and no timestamp); "Show Rank" doesn't exist and HFSE uses no honor tiers. Timetable/"Today 8:00–9:00" has no scheduling model at all (`attendance_daily.period_id` is a bare uuid, no FK, always NULL, reserved for a Scheduling module that was never built; `sections.schedule` is only `morning | afternoon | whole_day`).

**Navigation — the Phase 7 over-reach and its correction (Phase 8).** Phase 7 repointed all three teaching modules' nav entries at `/classroom`; this was **wrong and was reverted**. It made a teacher already in Attendance go Classes → class → Attendance tab → Open register → grid: four steps for a task done every morning. Being in a module already declares intent. The corrected model puts the handoff at the **row** level, per role:

| Role                                             | Lists show        | Clicking a class lands on                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| teacher (FCA or subject)                         | their own classes | the matching **Classroom tab** (`/attendance` → Attendance, `/markbook` → Grades, `/evaluation` → Write-ups) |
| academic_coordinator / school_admin / superadmin | all classes       | the **module's own** surface (register, `?grading.section=` sheets list, write-up roster) — unchanged        |

Teachers live in Classroom; oversight works _across_ classes so the module stays their home. The teacher-vs-oversight branch reads `scope.isOversight` from the shared resolver, never a fresh inline role check. **The three section lists were kept, not retired** — retire only once Classroom demonstrably covers their use. Classroom's own list rows land on Overview. The Classroom Attendance tab keeps its summary + "Open the attendance sheet" button (landing there and clicking through is intended, not a bug). `/markbook/sections/[id]` is a redirect stub to `/classroom/[id]` — the class page is genuinely its successor. `/sis/sections` is untouched: structural/destructive config (create, rename, delete, change level) does not belong beside "take attendance".

**Behaviour change to watch:** `/markbook/sections` previously applied **no** teacher scoping — it showed every user every section (the only outlier of the three). It is now scoped via the same resolver, so a teacher sees only their assigned classes. Those extra rows were unopenable anyway (RLS blocks the sheets), and without the fix the row handoff would 404.

**Deferred, deliberately:** ~~(a) a subject-teacher-only user still sees Attendance and Evaluation module tiles that are dead ends~~ — **closed**: `lib/sidebar/module-visibility.ts` (`hiddenModulesForTeacher` / `isHiddenModuleHref`) now narrows the module offers for a teacher holding no `form_adviser` row, applied across the switchers, the account shortcuts, the Cmd+K palette and the home quick-action row (that last one was a no-op until 2026-07-30 — see KD #163's knock-on note and `__tests__/home/quick-actions.test.ts`). Being an adviser ANYWHERE is enough; per-section capability stays Classroom's job. (b) retiring the three section lists; (c) embedding the attendance grid / score-entry grid inside Classroom tabs (they currently link out) — the two layouts are the same width so the old viewport objection was overstated, but it needs the grids extracted into shared components.

**Correction to this KD's own text (2026-07-30):** the paragraph above and `module-visibility.ts`'s header both asserted the dead-end tiles were "not a data leak — RLS and the page guards hold." That was **false for two attendance pages**, which read via the service client with no capability check of their own. Fixed in KD #163; the claim now holds.

**Second correction (2026-07-31) — the Phase 8 ROW handoff is reversed for teachers.** The table above sends a teacher clicking a class in Attendance or Evaluation to the matching Classroom tab. That is undone in KD #170: those rows now go straight to the register and the write-up roster, like oversight. Classroom's Attendance tab's own primary button is "Open the attendance sheet", so marking a class cost two clicks where the row already named the destination — and this KD's own rationale, _"being in a module already declares intent"_, argues for the shorter path. Phase 8 applied that reasoning to the module NAV but still routed rows through Classroom. Markbook is unaffected: a teacher has no Markbook "Sections" nav item, so there is no handoff there. **Classroom itself is unchanged and still the whole-class view** — it is simply no longer on the path to the day's marking.

**Update (2026-08-21) — the Overview now says who runs the class.** Mr Ace: _"there is no trace of who teaches who/FCA etc in the classroom/[id] page"_. It was worse than absent: the form adviser was named in exactly **one** situation — the "No form adviser assigned" Health row — so assigning an adviser made the page go **silent** about them. `lib/classroom/staff.ts` + `components/classroom/classroom-staff-panel.tsx` render the adviser, then one row per subject the class takes with its teacher or **"No teacher"**, then a covered-count footer.

- ⚠ **The gaps are indigo, not red.** AY2026 has almost no teacher assignments, so destructive tinting would paint nearly every class red on day one — a true statement that reads as a broken system. Semantic colour has to survive the empty state (design-system §9.3).
- **Every capability sees the panel** — section setup is not RLS-restricted data. Only oversight gets the "Manage teachers" link to `/sis/sections/[id]?tab=teachers`, which is where assignment already lives; **no second copy of that surface was built.**
- **Relief editing was deliberately left alone here.** ⚠ The reason recorded at the time was that `relief-cover-dates.md` is a parked plan that reworks it — but KD #184 has since **removed** the date window that plan was built around, so treat the plan as probably obsolete and re-read it against KD #184 before reviving anything from it.
- **`lib/classroom/staff.ts` is classified `name` in the reader registry** (KD #184's act-vs-name split). It reads `relief_teacher_user_id` on purpose, but renders "R. Fernandez · Jenny Wong covering" — **holder first, never displacing the name of record.**
- It is also the **first entry in `module-inpage-link-reachability`'s ALLOWLIST**, which had been empty by design: a static scan cannot see a `canManage` gate, so a conditionally-rendered link reads as unreachable.

### KD #172

**Classroom Timeline — group by day, collapse runs** (2026-07-31; no migration). The tab rendered ~50 `audit_log` rows as one flat slab. Its worst property was not density: the **loudest element, a coloured pill, was usually the same word forty times running**, so repetition became the dominant visual and the eye got no signal at all. Most rows carried no detail either — `auditContextSummary` returns `'—'` when a context has nothing summarisable (KD #121), so a typical row was a badge, an email and a full timestamp arranged around **no information**.

**The fix is one idea: treat repetition as repetition.** Seven write-ups saved back-to-back by one person is ONE row carrying a count and a time span, expandable to the individual entries — not seven rows that look identical. Everything else follows from making room for that:

- **Days become sticky headings**, so rows keep only a time. Fifty full dates become a few headings and fifty short times.
- **The badge becomes a dot on a spine.** Colour still encodes the kind; the action name becomes the row's own text, which is what the reader was after. It is also what finally makes the page read as a timeline rather than a list that happens to be in date order.
- **The actor gets a display name and an initials circle**, resolved through the cached staff map (`getStaffDisplayEntries`), instead of the same email on forty rows. An unknown email falls back to itself, so a cron or a departed account still renders truthfully.
- **A `'—'` line is omitted rather than printed.** An empty event should be short, not padded.
- **Filter chips per kind** — Grades · Write-ups · Roster · Sheets — matching the four categories the page's own copy already named. Chips count **events, not runs**, so a chip cannot promise more than clicking it shows.

**Three rules in `lib/classroom/timeline.ts` (pure, unit-tested) that a naive version gets wrong:**

1. **Days are SINGAPORE days** (`sgDate`, KD #32). Grouping on the raw UTC stamp files everything before 08:00 SGT under the previous day — most of a school morning, and exactly when a register gets marked.
2. **Collapsing is CONSECUTIVE-only.** A, B, A stays three runs; merging them would claim two things happened together when something else happened in between, and that ordering is what a timeline exists to carry.
3. **A run never spans midnight**, because days are grouped first — which is what the day headings promise.

`kindForAction` is **prefix-matched rather than enumerated**, so a new `sheet.*` or `evaluation.*` lands in the right bucket without anyone editing it, and anything unrecognised files under `other` rather than being dropped — losing an event would be far worse than a generic heading. Labels and summaries still go through the shared humanizer (KD #121); the page is now a thin shell that fetches, resolves names and hands off. The capability guard is unchanged (every capability may open this tab — the data is not `is_adviser_for_section` gated), as is the deliberate exclusion of per-mark attendance and its reasoning. Mockup approved before any JSX: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-31-classroom-timeline-mockup.html`.

**Known trade-off:** collapsing puts exact per-event times one click away. Right for a teacher scanning "what happened in my class"; if this page is ever wanted as an audit trail where every timestamp matters at a glance, expanded-by-default is the switch.

**Test hardening from this work** (`__tests__/auth/nav-route-consistency-all-modules.test.ts`): the module-root map now derives from `SIDEBAR_REGISTRY[m].primaryHref` — what the real switcher filters on — instead of a hand-maintained copy that drifted the moment a module was added. And a new **direction C** asserts every module root has an explicit `ROUTE_ACCESS` rule: verified by experiment that deleting Classroom's row left directions A and B **green**, because `isRouteAllowed` returns `true` for an unmatched prefix, so both passed vacuously while every role — including `admissions` and `p_file_officer` — could open `/classroom`. The highest-risk mistake when adding a module was invisible to the guard meant to catch it.

### KD #181

**A teacher sees a student's medical, learning needs and home contacts inside Classroom — not by being let into Records** (2026-08-09; no migration). Asked for at the 2026-07-31 academics training by three people in five forms: Christina (16:08, allergies and special instructions, "so we don't have to look for several folders"), Melissa (21:53, medical alerts and learning difficulties, "for us teachers"), Chandana (22:36, parents' contact details).

**The obvious build was wrong.** All of it already existed on `/records/students/[studentNumber]`, so "just let teachers open that page" is one line of `ROUTE_ACCESS`. That page also renders NRIC, passport number and expiry, the immigration pass, the home address, the family's payment scheme and any discount code, plus the Edit sheets. Surfacing an allergy is not a reason to hand a subject teacher a child's passport number. The link to that page was removed from the roster three days earlier for the adjacent reason (KD #174), and re-adding it would have undone that fix rather than completed it.

**What shipped instead** is a `Sheet` on the Classroom roster — `View details` per row, and the student's **name** as the same trigger for anyone who cannot open the permanent record, which gives the name somewhere to go again. Inside: three tabs (Medical · Learning · Contacts) over `StudentDetailsSource`, a hand-written twenty-five-field type. The narrowness is the security model: a column added to the admissions table cannot reach a teacher by widening a spread, and `__tests__/classroom/student-details.test.ts` asserts the serialised payload contains none of `nric` / `passport` / `homeaddress` / `payment` / `discount` / `withdraw`.

**Authorisation is the section, twice.** `loadClassroomAccess` for the capability, then a `section_students` lookup proving the student is on _that_ roster. This is why no "does this teacher teach this student" resolver had to exist: the section answers both questions, and pairing your own section id with a guessed student number gets a 404. Every failure is a 404 rather than a 403 — telling the two apart would confirm the student number is real. `canReadRoster` is the bar, so **subject teachers see it too**; Melissa asked as one.

**Three design decisions worth keeping:**

1. **The safety strip sits OUTSIDE the tabs.** Tabs were the right call for four topics — they were also Mr Ace's — but a peanut allergy behind a click is worse than no tabs at all. A medical flag renders above the tab list and stays on screen whichever tab is open. `student-details-sheet.test.tsx` asserts it survives a tab change, scoped to the strip, because the same sentence also appears inside the Medical tab and an unscoped query would pass on the tab's copy alone.
2. **Tabs carry a marker dot, and it never lies.** Most students have nothing in either Medical or Learning, so without a marker a teacher opens every tab to discover they are empty. The dot is driven only by content that survived the junk filter, so a tab promising something never opens onto "NA".
3. **Which tab opens is data-dependent** — Medical if there is anything medical, then Learning, otherwise Contacts. Landing on an empty Medical tab for the ~95% who have nothing would make the drawer read as broken.

**`isRecorded` is the load-bearing pure function.** `additionalLearningNeeds` is a free-text box on the enrolment form and a parent with nothing to declare types something anyway. Production, 2026-08-09: **65 of 498 applications populated, 35 of those junk** (`NA`, `N/A`, `N.A.`, `Na`, `None`, `No`, `-`) and **32 real** — ADHD ×3, autism spectrum disorder ×2, speech and language delay, ABA therapy, shadow support, SPED, EIP. Matching is on the whole value with punctuation stripped, never a prefix: `Nanette assists in class` and `nonverbal in large groups` both start with a junk spelling and are both real.

⚠ **The structured medical booleans are nearly empty, and one row proves why that matters.** 4 allergies, 2 food allergies, 3 eczema, 1 asthma, and **zero** epilepsy / diabetes / heart conditions across 498 applications — while one parent described their child's focal epilepsy in the learning-needs box with the epilepsy tick-box unticked. So a free-text medical note alone raises the safety strip with no condition ticked; keying `hasMedical` off the booleans would have hidden the one case that matters most. Paracetamol consent is deliberately excluded from `hasMedical`: 25 students carry a consent answer and nothing else, and a red strip on all of them would teach people to ignore the strip.

**The query lives inside `SheetContent`, not the outer component.** Radix mounts that subtree only while open, so a closed roster of forty students runs zero queries and needs no `QueryClient` in scope. With `useQuery` in the outer component every roster render demanded a provider — caught by `classroom-roster-table.test.tsx` failing, which is the test doing its job.

**Deliberately not built:** any edit path (the office owns this data and the Records editor already exists — whether teachers may add to it is question 3 in the rewritten message to Christina); the **Grades tab** carrying Ms Koh's at-risk detail, reserved in the tab strip and belonging to T1; and **Conduct**, which needs the disciplinary table that does not exist yet (T5). The tab strip is built so both slot in without rework.

**Live-failure correction (2026-08-09, same day it shipped).** The drawer 500'd on the first student it was ever opened on, and would have done so on **every student in the school**: `isRecorded` was typed `string | null` and called `.toLowerCase()`, but **all four mobile columns on the admissions table are NUMERIC** — `motherMobile` is a `number` on 498 of 498 AY2026 rows, and `fatherMobile` (474), `guardianMobile` (120) and `contactPersonNumber` (498) likewise. The type was a guess about the database; the database disagreed. `isRecorded` and `recorded` now take `unknown` and coerce with `String()`, and `StudentDetailsSource` types those four as `string | number | null` — describing what arrives, not what ought to. Verified by shaping all 498 production rows: **0 throw**.

**Two things this teaches beyond the fix:**

1. **These columns will become heterogeneously typed**, exactly like `residenceHistory`. The parent portal writes numbers; `lib/schemas/sis.ts`'s `optionalPhone` is `z.string()`, so the SIS profile editor writes strings back. Any future reader must coerce, never assume — and a type probe must scan **every** row rather than the first non-null one. `lib/sis/queries.ts` still types these `string | null` on `StudentDetail`; nothing there calls a string method on them today, so it is a latent trap rather than a live bug, and it is recorded here rather than widened blind.
2. **The error surface was the real defect.** The first report was a screenshot reading "This student's details could not be loaded" — true, and useless. The route now names the step it died on (`capability` / `student lookup` / `roster check` / `admissions row`), returns it in the body, and outside production appends the exception message; the panel prints `Error 500 · admissions row — value.toLowerCase is not a function`. That one line ended a debugging loop that three rounds of reasoning from the outside had not. **When a failure is only ever reported as a screenshot, the screenshot has to carry the diagnosis.** A 404 gets its own wording instead — "This student is not on this class list" — because that one has a cause a teacher can act on.

Same pass, a second defect found by inspection rather than by failing: the roster check ignored its own query error, so a `maybeSingle()` failure (which is what more than one non-withdrawn row for a section produces) was read as "not on this roster" and returned 404. It now throws and is logged. Worth knowing that H220056 legitimately holds two active `section_students` rows in two different sections, so the shape is not hypothetical.

### KD #182

**The form adviser's at-risk list lives on Classroom → Grades** (2026-08-09; no migration). The other half of Ms Koh's training ask (55:10): _"Can this system actually help to flag out students who are scoring at risk... then the subject teacher **or the FCA** got to contact the parents."_ The subject-teacher half shipped as a lookup on the grading sheet (KD #179) where every number was already on screen; an adviser marks no subject, has no sheet, and so had nothing.

**Three decisions carry it:**

1. **The student is the unit, not the subject.** An adviser rings a parent once, about a child. One row per student, with every subject that slipped inside it — a per-subject list would put the same child in front of them four times. This is also the correction KD #179 already had to make once: Koh asked to "flag out students", plural, and the first build gave her a per-row column.
2. **Adviser and oversight only** (`canReadReportCard`, not `canReadRoster`) — the same line the report card draws, for the same reason. This carries every subject's marks for the whole class, which a subject teacher has no business seeing; theirs is the lookup on their own sheet. Each of Koh's two people now has the view their own job needs.
3. **Two views in one panel, not a sheet on a sheet.** The list, then one student with what fell and the numbers to ring, and a link back — the shape the attendance and grading lookups already use, down to the words on the button. A nested dialog was written first and the component test caught it; nesting traps focus twice and makes the back gesture meaningless.

**Why the button hides in Term 1**: there is no earlier term to have fallen from, so it would open onto a permanent nothing.

**`rankAtRisk` reuses `GRADE_ALERT_THRESHOLD` and `ALERT_METRICS`** rather than restating them. A teacher who sees a five-point drop flagged on their own sheet must find that student on the adviser's list too, or the two surfaces disagree about what "at risk" means.

**Two subtleties in the comparison, both tested:**

- **The baseline is the most recent term that actually carries a mark**, not simply the previous term. A subject can go unmarked for a whole term — a sheet created late, a subject starting mid-year — and taking that empty term as the baseline would say nothing about a student who has in fact fallen since their last real mark.
- **Missing data is not a signal.** No prior term (a late enrollee) and no mark yet this term both produce nothing. "We cannot tell" is not "at risk", and a list that flagged every new child is a list an adviser stops opening.

**`loadSectionAtRisk` calls `loadPriorTermGrades` once per subject** rather than issuing one wide query, deliberately. That helper is transfer-safe in a way that is easy to get wrong quietly: a mid-year transfer (KD #67) withdraws the old enrolment row, so a student's Term 1 marks sit under a row this section has never seen, and it resolves them by `student_id` across every section's sheets for the subject. Re-deriving that here would duplicate the subtlest query in the codebase and let the copies drift. Ten subjects is ten parallel calls on a panel opened by hand.

**An empty list says "Nobody needs a look"**, not "no results" — an adviser seeing a failed-search empty state would reasonably conclude the feature was broken rather than that their class is fine.

**Still open on this ask:** whether anyone should be _notified_ when a student is flagged (the bell carries only change requests today), and what the threshold should be — five points is a display heuristic nobody at HFSE has yet had an opinion about. Both are questions 2 and 3 in the message to Koh, still unsent.
